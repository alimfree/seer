Chronic Care Management (CCM) is one of the most accessible revenue programs for primary care practices, yet adoption remains surprisingly low. Many practices either do not bill for CCM at all or bill inconsistently because they find the time tracking, documentation, and consent requirements confusing. This guide covers the specific CPT codes, documentation standards, and operational workflows you need to bill CCM correctly in 2026 — including how to stack CCM with RPM for maximum per-patient revenue.

## CCM Codes at a Glance

There are three primary CCM codes your practice should know. Each has distinct time thresholds, billing requirements, and reimbursement rates.

| CPT Code | Description | Time Requirement | Who Can Bill | 2026 Medicare Rate (Approx.) |
|----------|-------------|-----------------|-------------|-------------------------------|
| 99490 | Standard CCM | 20 minutes/month | Clinical staff under physician | $62–$64 |
| 99491 | Complex CCM (physician time) | 30 minutes/month | Physician or qualified HCP | $87–$93 |
| 99437 | Each additional 30 min (add-on to 99491) | 30 minutes/month | Physician or qualified HCP | $75–$80 |

A fourth code, 99439, serves as the add-on to 99490 for each additional 20 minutes of clinical staff time. It reimburses approximately $47-$50 per unit.

| CPT Code | Description | Time Requirement | Who Can Bill | 2026 Medicare Rate (Approx.) |
|----------|-------------|-----------------|-------------|-------------------------------|
| 99439 | Additional 20 min (add-on to 99490) | 20 minutes/month | Clinical staff under physician | $47–$50 |

### Choosing Between 99490 and 99491

The choice between these two codes depends on who is spending the time and the complexity of the patient's conditions.

**Use 99490 when:**
- Clinical staff (RNs, MAs, care coordinators) are providing the bulk of the care management time.
- The care plan is relatively stable and the patient's conditions are being managed, not actively adjusted.
- You want to leverage lower-cost staff for care coordination activities.

**Use 99491 when:**
- The physician or qualified healthcare professional (NP, PA) is personally providing the care management time.
- The patient has complex medical needs requiring physician-level decision-making each month.
- Medical decision-making involves adjusting treatment plans, reconciling conflicting specialist recommendations, or managing medication interactions.

> **Revenue insight:** Many practices default to 99490 because it uses clinical staff time, which feels more cost-effective. But for patients who genuinely require monthly physician involvement, billing 99491 at $87-$93 instead of 99490 at $62-$64 increases revenue by roughly 40% per patient per month. Audit your CCM patient panel quarterly to identify patients who should be upgraded to 99491.

## Time Tracking Requirements

Time tracking is the single biggest compliance risk in CCM billing. CMS requires that the time spent on CCM activities be documented in the medical record, and the time must meet the minimum threshold for the code billed.

### What Counts as CCM Time

Not all patient-related work counts toward CCM time. Here is what qualifies:

- Reviewing and revising the patient's comprehensive care plan.
- Communicating with the patient or caregiver (phone, secure messaging, patient portal).
- Coordinating care with pharmacies, specialists, labs, hospitals, home health agencies, and community services.
- Managing transitions of care (e.g., post-hospitalization follow-up).
- Medication management and reconciliation.
- Documenting CCM activities in the EHR.

### What Does NOT Count

- Time spent on separately billable services (E/M visits, RPM time, telehealth visits).
- Time spent on administrative tasks not directly related to the patient's care plan (scheduling appointments, filing paperwork).
- Time spent by staff who are not clinically qualified (front desk, billing staff).
- Travel time.

### Time Tracking Best Practices

| Method | Pros | Cons |
|--------|------|------|
| Manual time logs (spreadsheet) | Low cost, easy to start | Error-prone, difficult to audit, staff forget to log |
| EHR-integrated timers | Accurate, auditable, links to patient record | Requires EHR configuration, may slow down workflow |
| Standalone CCM platform | Purpose-built, comprehensive reporting | Additional cost, may not integrate with all EHRs |
| Automated activity tracking | Captures time passively, reduces staff burden | May overcapture — requires validation |

Whichever method you choose, the documentation must include the date, the staff member who performed the activity, the specific activity performed, and the duration. Vague entries like "CCM care coordination — 25 min" are insufficient. The note should specify what was coordinated and with whom.

## Patient Consent for CCM

Medicare requires patient consent before you can bill CCM services. This consent requirement is distinct from the general consent patients sign at intake.

### What the Consent Must Include

1. The patient understands that CCM services will be provided and billed to Medicare.
2. The patient understands that only one practitioner can bill CCM for them at a time.
3. The patient has the right to revoke consent at any time.
4. The patient understands their cost-sharing obligation (typically 20% coinsurance for Original Medicare).

Verbal consent is acceptable for Medicare but must be documented with the date, who obtained it, and what was discussed. Written consent is preferred and required by some commercial payers. You can bill starting from the consent date, though most practices begin the following month for simplicity.

> **Practice tip:** Obtain CCM consent during an annual wellness visit or routine office visit. This is the most natural time to discuss chronic condition management with the patient and avoids the awkwardness of a standalone consent call.

## Documentation Requirements

CMS requires a comprehensive, patient-centered care plan for every CCM patient covering the problem list, expected outcomes, measurable treatment goals, planned interventions, medication list, and how providers will coordinate. This plan must be reviewed and updated at least annually. Each month you bill, document each CCM activity, total time, staff credentials, and any care plan updates.

## Stacking CCM with RPM

CCM and RPM are separate service categories with separate CPT codes. CMS allows you to bill both for the same patient in the same month, provided the time and activities are distinct.

### Rules for Stacking

1. **Time cannot be double-counted.** If a nurse spends 20 minutes reviewing RPM data and adjusting a care plan, that time counts toward either RPM (99457) or CCM (99490), but not both.
2. **Activities must be distinct.** RPM time is specifically for reviewing device-transmitted data and communicating with the patient about that data. CCM time is for broader care coordination, care plan management, and non-device-related communication.
3. **Both require independent documentation.** You need separate time logs and activity notes for RPM and CCM.

### Revenue Projection: CCM Alone vs. CCM + RPM

Here is what monthly revenue looks like per patient under different billing scenarios.

| Billing Scenario | Codes Billed | Monthly Revenue (Medicare) |
|-----------------|-------------|---------------------------|
| CCM only (standard) | 99490 | $62–$64 |
| CCM only (complex) | 99491 | $87–$93 |
| RPM only (monitoring + clinical time) | 99454 + 99457 | $108–$120 |
| CCM + RPM (standard CCM) | 99490 + 99454 + 99457 | $170–$184 |

### Practical Stacking Workflow

Here is how to operationalize CCM + RPM stacking:

1. **Separate your time logs.** Use distinct time-tracking categories for RPM activities and CCM activities. Your staff should select "RPM" or "CCM" at the start of each documented activity.
2. **Define activity boundaries.** Create a reference sheet for staff that lists which activities count toward RPM time and which count toward CCM time. For example:
   - Reviewing blood glucose data from the RPM device = RPM time.
   - Calling the patient's endocrinologist to coordinate insulin adjustments = CCM time.
   - Calling the patient to discuss a blood glucose spike seen on the RPM device = RPM time.
   - Updating the patient's medication list after a specialist visit = CCM time.
3. **Bill RPM and CCM on separate claim lines.** They are separate CPT codes and should appear as separate line items on the same claim.
4. **Monitor for time overlap.** If your documentation shows a nurse spent 20 minutes on an activity that could be classified as either RPM or CCM, you need to assign it to one category — not split it across both.

## Common CCM Billing Mistakes

**Billing without documented consent.** This is the fastest way to trigger a recoupment. Always verify consent is documented before submitting the first CCM claim.

**Double-counting time across CCM and RPM.** If audited, you must be able to prove that each minute of time was allocated to only one service category.

**Not meeting the time threshold.** If your staff logs 18 minutes of CCM time in a month, you cannot bill 99490 (which requires 20 minutes). Do not round up. Either ensure the threshold is met or do not bill for that month.

**Failing to maintain the care plan.** A stale care plan from two years ago does not meet CMS requirements. The care plan must be current, comprehensive, and reflective of the patient's actual conditions and treatment.

**Ignoring the single-provider rule.** Only one provider can bill CCM for a patient in a given month. If your patient is receiving CCM from a specialist and your practice simultaneously, only one of you can bill. Coordinate with the patient and other providers to avoid duplicate billing.
