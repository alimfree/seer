## What Is Changing for RPM in the 2026 Physician Fee Schedule

Every year, the CMS Physician Fee Schedule (PFS) Final Rule reshapes the economics and compliance landscape for Remote Patient Monitoring. The CY 2026 PFS Final Rule introduces several changes that directly affect how primary care practices bill, document, and operate their RPM programs -- particularly those focused on chronic disease management for diabetic patients.

This post covers the key changes, what they mean in practical terms, and what your practice needs to update before January 1, 2026.

## Reimbursement Rate Adjustments

The 2026 conversion factor and relative value unit (RVU) updates affect RPM reimbursement across all codes. CMS has continued its pattern of modest adjustments, but the cumulative impact on practice revenue is meaningful, especially for high-volume RPM programs.

The conversion factor reduction of approximately 2.2% reflects the broader budget neutrality adjustments CMS applies across all physician services. While the per-code decrease appears small, practices with large RPM populations will see a measurable revenue impact.

> **What this means for your budget:** A practice with 200 RPM patients billing 99454 and 99457 monthly will see roughly $5,600 less in annual RPM revenue from rate changes alone. Factor this into your 2026 financial projections now.

## New and Clarified Requirements

Beyond rate changes, the 2026 PFS Final Rule includes several policy clarifications and new requirements that affect RPM program operations.

### Supervision Requirements Clarification

CMS has provided additional guidance on the supervision level required for RPM services. The 2026 rule clarifies that:

- **99453 and 99454** continue to require general supervision -- the supervising physician does not need to be present in the office when the service is performed, but must be available to provide assistance and direction.
- **99457 and 99458** require general supervision as well. Clinical staff (RNs, MAs, LPNs under state scope of practice) can perform the interactive communication and data review, with the billing practitioner available by phone or other means.

This clarification is significant for practices that use remote clinical staff for RPM management. As long as the supervising physician is reachable and has established the care plan, remote clinical staff can perform the monthly management services.

### Practitioner Order Documentation

CMS has reinforced that an individualized RPM treatment plan or order must be documented for each patient. The 2026 guidance specifies that the order must include:

- The specific condition being monitored (ICD-10 code)
- The physiologic parameter to be monitored (e.g., blood glucose)
- The type of device to be used
- The expected duration of monitoring
- The frequency of data review by clinical staff

Generic standing orders that apply blanket RPM enrollment to all patients with a given diagnosis are explicitly insufficient. Each order must reflect an individualized clinical decision.

> **Action required:** Review your current RPM order templates. If you are using a generic order that does not specify the individual patient's monitoring parameters and duration, update it before January 2026.

### Consolidated Billing for Skilled Nursing Facility Patients

The 2026 rule reaffirms that RPM services are subject to the SNF consolidated billing exclusion. Patients in a Part A SNF stay cannot be separately billed for RPM services. Practices must verify the patient's setting before billing each month.

This is particularly relevant for diabetic patients who may transition between home and SNF settings. Your billing workflow should include a monthly eligibility check that flags patients in SNF stays.

## Telehealth and RPM: Evolving Intersections

The relationship between telehealth policy and RPM continues to evolve in the 2026 rule. Several provisions affect practices that use both telehealth and RPM for their diabetic patient populations.

### Telehealth Flexibilities Extension

CMS has extended certain pandemic-era telehealth flexibilities through 2026, including:

- Audio-only visits can still be used for RPM-related patient communication under 99457 when the patient cannot access video technology
- Geographic restrictions on originating sites remain relaxed through 2026
- The patient's home continues to qualify as an originating site

### RPM vs. RTM: Continued Distinction

CMS continues to maintain the distinction between Remote Physiologic Monitoring (RPM, 9945x codes) and Remote Therapeutic Monitoring (RTM, 9847x codes). For diabetic patients, RPM is the appropriate code family because blood glucose is a physiologic parameter, not a therapeutic response metric.

Practices should not use RTM codes for glucose monitoring. Using the wrong code family is a billing error that triggers denials and can prompt post-payment audits.

### Integration with Chronic Care Management

The 2026 rule maintains the provision that RPM and CCM services can be billed for the same patient in the same month, provided the time documented for each service is distinct and non-overlapping. This is an important revenue consideration for diabetic patients who qualify for both programs.

However, the documentation requirements are strict:

- Time logs must clearly separate RPM activities from CCM activities
- A single phone call that covers both RPM data review and CCM care coordination must be split by time and documented under each code appropriately
- The same minute of staff time cannot be counted toward both RPM and CCM

## What Practices Need to Update Before January 2026

Based on the 2026 rule changes, here is a prioritized checklist of operational updates.

### Immediate (Complete by December 2025)

1. **Update fee schedules and financial projections.** Load the 2026 rates into your billing system and adjust revenue forecasts.
2. **Review RPM order templates.** Ensure each order includes patient-specific parameters, device type, monitoring duration, and review frequency.
3. **Verify staff supervision documentation.** Confirm that your records identify the supervising practitioner for each RPM service.
4. **Update patient cost-sharing estimates.** Adjust the estimated copay/coinsurance amounts on your consent forms to reflect 2026 rates.

### Near-Term (Complete by Q1 2026)

5. **Audit SNF patient exclusions.** Implement or verify a monthly eligibility check that prevents RPM billing for patients in SNF stays.
6. **Review RPM/CCM time separation.** If you bill both programs, audit a sample of dual-enrolled patients to verify time documentation is properly separated.
7. **Train clinical staff on order requirements.** Ensure clinicians understand that generic standing orders are not sufficient and that each RPM order requires individualization.

### Ongoing

8. **Monitor MAC-specific guidance.** Your Medicare Administrative Contractor may issue Local Coverage Determinations (LCDs) or articles that further refine RPM billing requirements in your jurisdiction.
9. **Track proposed rules for CY 2027.** CMS typically publishes the proposed rule in July. Early engagement helps practices prepare for future changes.

## Preparing for Continued Regulatory Evolution

The RPM regulatory landscape has changed meaningfully every year since CMS first created the RPM code family. The 2026 changes are incremental rather than transformational, but they continue a trend toward greater specificity in documentation and ordering requirements.

Practices that invest in compliance infrastructure -- standardized order templates, automated time tracking, eligibility verification -- absorb these annual changes with minimal disruption. Practices that rely on ad hoc processes face an annual scramble to retrain staff and update workflows.

### Key Trends to Watch

- **Value-based payment models:** CMS continues to explore integrating RPM into Alternative Payment Models (APMs). Practices participating in MSSP ACOs or other APMs should monitor how RPM utilization is treated in quality and cost benchmarking.
- **Device interoperability standards:** CMS has signaled interest in requiring certified device standards for RPM in future rulemaking. Practices should prioritize devices that support standardized data formats (e.g., IEEE 11073, FHIR-compatible).
- **Artificial intelligence in monitoring:** As AI-driven alert systems become more common in RPM platforms, CMS will likely issue guidance on how AI-assisted monitoring affects documentation and supervision requirements. The clinical staff member must still exercise independent clinical judgment -- AI triage does not substitute for human review.

> **Planning tip:** Build your 2026 RPM budget with a 2-3% rate reduction buffer. CMS has reduced the conversion factor in most recent years, and expecting flat or increased reimbursement is not supported by the trend data.
